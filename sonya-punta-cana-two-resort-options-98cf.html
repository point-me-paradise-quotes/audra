You land at Punta Cana at 2:35 p.m. on a Sunday. The Dominican Republic runs on Atlantic Standard Time year-round and doesn't observe daylight saving, so in October you're one hour ahead of Dallas. Set your watch on the plane and forget about it.

PUJ is a genuinely pleasant airport — open-air, thatched roofs, and small enough that you're through it faster than you'd expect. Both resorts sit within about half an hour of the terminal, which is one of the real arguments for this stretch of coast: you're at the beach before dinner rather than after dark.

How the two travel days shape up

  • Sunday is an early start. A 9:01 a.m. departure means arriving at DFW around 7:00 a.m., which means leaving home before six. Worth knowing when you plan Saturday night.
  • Friday is the easy one. A 3:41 p.m. departure works comfortably against a noon check-out — leave the resort around noon, at the airport by 12:45, three clear hours before the flight. You keep the whole last morning on the beach.

That's an unusually good return time for this route. Most Punta Cana returns are morning flights that cost you the final day.

Option A — Catalonia Royal BávaroCabeza de Toro · Superior Junior Suite · $3,184.99

Cabeza de Toro is the quiet middle ground of this coast — a headland between Punta Cana proper and the long Bávaro strip, with a marina, a handful of seafood restaurants, and noticeably less going on than either neighbor. The water here is shallow and calm, protected by the reef, and the beach doesn't carry the vendor traffic that Bávaro does.

The Royal is the adults-only property inside the larger Catalonia Bávaro complex. You're booked into a Superior Junior Suite, which is the category your rate is confirmed in.

Exchange privileges

Your stay includes full access to the facilities at three neighboring Palladium properties:

  • Grand Palladium Punta Cana Resort & Spa
  • Grand Palladium Select Bávaro Resort & Spa
  • Grand Palladium Palace Resort & Spa

Those are family resorts, so expect a different atmosphere the moment you cross over — which is precisely why having your own adults-only side to come back to is worth something.

What the all-inclusive covers

  • All meals.
  • Domestic drinks.
  • Non-motorized water sports.
  • Entertainment.
  • Free Wi-Fi in designated areas.
  • Taxes and tips.
Read that list carefully — I did. The supplier specifies domestic drinks and Wi-Fi in designated areas, not premium spirits and not property-wide coverage. TRS properties typically pour better than that at their own bars, and I suspect this listing understates it. But I'm not going to promise you top-shelf and airport-grade Wi-Fi in your room on the strength of a suspicion. I'm confirming both with the resort directly and I'll tell you what they say.

If you're celebrating a honeymoon

Palladium adds a welcome package at no cost, but it has to be noted on the reservation before travel:

  • 15% discount on unlimited massages.
  • Fruit basket and a bottle of champagne.
  • Room upgrade, subject to availability.
The catch: proof of marriage at check-in, dated no more than two months before your stay. That's a tighter window than most resorts ask for — if your certificate is older than that, these amenities won't be honored and we shouldn't count on them.

Cancellation — the one real difference in risk

This property carries a two-night cancellation penalty. The supplier lists a cancellation date of October 7, 2026 attached to that penalty. Given your balance is due August 27, that means your money is committed well before the penalty window even opens. Travel protection is the only thing that changes that math, and Funjet's own note is explicit that their Travel Protection Plus does not cover penalties on non-refundable room rates. I'll get you the exact terms in writing before you deposit — this is not a detail to take on trust.
